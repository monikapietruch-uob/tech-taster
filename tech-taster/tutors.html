Run this taster with a group

These notes are for a tutor or teacher who wants to use the taster in a session of about an hour. They are a first draft.

Before the session

  • Open the taster on the network the group will use. It needs a web browser and nothing else: no accounts and nothing to install.
  • It works on phones, tablets and laptops. On a phone, the Add buttons mean nobody has to type brackets.
  • Do the three tasks yourself once. It takes about five minutes.
  • On a shared computer, open the page in a private window, so each person starts fresh.

A one-hour plan

Time What happens What to count
5 minutes Everyone answers one question on a slip of paper, with no name: “Could you do a tech course?” from 1 (no) to 5 (yes). The answers
40 minutes The three tasks, at each person’s own speed. Working in pairs is fine. Who starts, and who finishes each task
5 minutes The same question again, on the other side of the slip. The answers
10 minutes What next: where this leads and who to talk to. Who asks for more information

The three tasks, with one answer for each

There is more than one right answer. Any program that puts the robot on the flag counts.

Task 1: Reach the flag

New word: command. The starter program stops one square short, on purpose. Most people fix it within a minute.

move()
move()
move()

Task 2: Turn the corner

New word: program. The point to draw out is that turn_left() turns the robot but does not move it.

move()
move()
turn_left()
move()

Task 3: Fix the bug

New word: bug. The starter program walks into a rock on line 2. People have to read the message, then plan a way round.

turn_left()
move()
turn_right()
move()
move()
move()

Mistakes you will see

What happens What the page says What you can say
Someone types Move() Use small letters. Write move(). Computers are fussy about capital letters. People are not.
Someone types move Add brackets at the end. Write move(). The brackets mean “do it now”.
They expect turn_left() to move the robot Not there yet. Turning and moving are two different commands.
Too many move() lines The robot cannot leave the world. Count the squares, then count the lines.

When someone is stuck

  • Ask them to press Run and say out loud what the robot did. The answer is usually in what they see.
  • Point to the Show a hint button before you give an answer. The last hint in task 3 is a full answer.
  • “Not there yet” is the message everyone sees first. It is part of the task, not a failure.

What the taster keeps

Nothing leaves the device. Progress is kept in that browser only. On a shared computer the next person sees the last person’s progress, which is why a private window helps.
